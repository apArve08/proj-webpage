Step 1 of 5 • Input Representation

Tokenization, Embeddings & Positional Encoding

Language models cannot read raw strings. Text is split into subword/word Tokens, mapped to integer IDs in a vocabulary, converted into dense semantic vectors (E), and summed with sinusoidal Positional Encodings (P) so the model knows word order.

Mathematical Formulation:
xi = Embed(tokeni) + PosEnc(i)
where xi ∈ ℝ4 (simplified 4D space)

Token-by-Token Vector Addition (4D Simplified)

Why Positional Encoding?

Without positional vectors, "The robot learned" and "learned robot The" look identical to self-attention. Sine/cosine waves give each position a unique geometric signature.

PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 100002i/d)
Step 2 of 5 • Contextual Routing

Scaled Dot-Product Self-Attention & Causal Masking

Each token projects its input vector into three distinct roles: Query (Q) "What am I looking for?", Key (K) "What label do I advertise?", and Value (V) "What content do I pass along?". The Causal Mask prevents tokens from peeking at future words.

Attention Equation:
Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax((QKT / √dk) + M) V

Step 3 of 5 • Deep Feature Refinement

Inside the Transformer Decoder Block

GPT stacks multiple identical Transformer blocks. Each block has two main sub-layers: Masked Multi-Head Self-Attention (communication between tokens) and a position-wise Feed-Forward MLP (computation within each token), wrapped in Pre-LayerNorm and Residual Skip Connections (+).

Layer 1: Syntax & Local Grammar
h' = x + Attention(LN₁(x))

Tensor Shape: [N, 4]

Residual Connections (x + SubLayer(x))

Creates a "gradient superhighway" during backpropagation and allows each layer to make incremental edits to a token's representation rather than overwriting it from scratch.

Feed-Forward MLP (4d Expansion)

Projects 4D → 16D with GELU non-linearity then back to 4D. Research shows FFN layers act as key-value memories storing factual associations learned during pre-training.

Step 4 of 5 • Vocabulary Projection & Softmax

Linear Output Head (LM Head) & Temperature Scaling

Only the final token's output vector from the last Transformer layer is needed to predict the next word. It multiplies against the Vocabulary Unembedding matrix (Wvocab) to produce raw Logits, which are divided by Temperature (T) and normalized via Softmax into probabilities.

Probability Distribution:
P(wi) = exp(zi / T) / Σj exp(zj / T)

Step 5 of 5 • The Autoregressive Loop

Iterative Token-by-Token Generation

GPT is autoregressive: after sampling one next token from Step 4's probability distribution, that token is appended to the input sequence, and the entire forward pass (Steps 1 → 4) repeats to generate the next token until an <EOS> token or length limit is reached.
